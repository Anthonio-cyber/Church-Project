# Moderator guide

You keep the community safe from harassment, spam, impersonation and abuse.
You do not handle counselling, and you do not handle safeguarding — both have
their own people and their own protections.

---

## The queue

**Moderator Portal → Reports.** Four views:

| View | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Open | Nobody has looked yet |
| Under review | Claimed, being worked |
| Escalated | Now with safeguarding — no longer yours |
| Closed | Resolved or dismissed |

Each report shows the reporter, the reported account, its status, how many prior
reports it has, and the reported message if one was attached.

---

## Working a report

1. **Claim** it, so two moderators do not duplicate work.
2. Read what was reported. Check the prior report count — a pattern matters more
   than a single incident.
3. Decide:
   - **Resolve** — you acted.
   - **Dismiss** — no action was needed.
   - **Escalate to safeguarding** — this is beyond moderation.

Every action needs a written reason of at least eight characters. That reason
is what the reporter sees when they are told their report was reviewed, so write
it for them.

---

## When to escalate rather than resolve

Escalate anything involving:

- abuse, threats or intimidation
- exploitation or coercion
- concern that someone may harm themselves
- the safety of a child or young person
- sexual misconduct
- financial exploitation of a member

Reports categorised as **sexual misconduct** or **threats** are escalated
automatically and never reach your queue.

**Escalating does not give you access to the resulting case.** That is
deliberate — you raise the alarm, safeguarding leads handle it. If that feels
like being cut out, it is worth remembering that the same boundary protects the
people you are escalating on behalf of.

---

## What you cannot see

- Private counselling conversations, or any counsellor's notes.
- Safeguarding case narratives, including for cases you escalated.
- A message reported from a counselling conversation — the queue shows that it
  was withheld and why.

None of this is a permissions oversight. A moderator handling community conduct
does not need pastoral confidences, so the role does not carry them.

---

## Blocks and reports are different things

A **block** is a member's own decision. It takes effect immediately, hides
existing conversation both ways, stops notifications and prevents further
requests. Members do not need your permission.

A **report** asks you to look. Someone may do both.

---

## If you are unsure

Escalate. A report that turns out not to need safeguarding costs a lead a few
minutes. A safeguarding matter left in the moderation queue costs something
that cannot be recovered.
